Designing Gamified Physiotherapy Exercises in VR

I designed and implemented interactive VR exercise systems and minigames which focus on making physiotherapy more engaging. This was done using gamified feedback, streaks, leaderboards and minigames.

VR Physiotherapy Exercise Bar

I designed an exercise bar that could support multiple types of physiotherapy exercises in VR. The bar communicates which movement to perform and how long the player should perform it.

Before starting an exercise, the player first measures their head sway. This establishes a baseline difficulty that can be used to evaluate their movement during the exercise.

I also implemented visual affordances around the player. The pulsing balls indicate the direction of the required movement: green for the left controller, blue for the right controller, and pink for head movement. These colors are consistent with the controller colors and movement illustrations used throughout the experience.

To make the exercises feel more like a game, I added feedback such as Great, Good and Perfect, supported by color, visual effects, and sound.

Passthrough Environment

I implemented a passthrough environment that uses mixed reality to keep the player's real surroundings visible while placing the exercise interface and interactive elements in world space. This provides an alternative to performing exercises inside a completely virtual environment.

Research

Research into how passthrough and fully virtual environments can affect exercise performance.

Minigames

Designing VR Physiotherapy Minigames

I also explored how traditional game mechanics could be adapted into exercises while still encouraging the intended physical movements.

Sword Minigame

The player has to step outside their central position and reach toward targets to slash them with two swords. This turns the required physical movement into a simple game challenge.

VR Physiotherapy Bow

I designed a bow mechanic where the left controller represents the bow and the right controller grabs the string.

Pulling the string farther increases the shot's strength, encouraging the player to maintain a straight posture while performing the movement.

Reflection

Looking back, I would add a handedness option so players could swap the bow and string-hand controls for left-handed use.

Making Physiotherapy Exercises Fun

During my VR minor, I partnered up with Physiotherapy professionals to create a VR game designed for folks with lower back pain.

I designed and implemented interactive VR exercise systems and minigames which focus on making physiotherapy more engaging. This was done using gamified feedback, streaks, leaderboards and minigames.

VR Physiotherapy Exercise Bar

I designed an exercise bar that could support multiple types of physiotherapy exercises in VR. The bar communicates which movement to perform and how long the player should perform it.

Before starting an exercise, the player first measures their head sway. This establishes the desired "difficulty" that can be used to evaluate their movement during the exercise.

I also implemented visual affordances around the player. I added pulsing balls which indicate the direction of the desired positions of each movement: green for the left controller, blue for the right controller, and pink for your head. These colors are consistent with the controller colors and movement illustrations used throughout the experience.

To make the exercises feel more like a game, I added feedback such as Great, Good and Perfect, supported by color, visual effects, and sound.

Environments

I implemented the ability to switch maps, including a passthrough environment that uses mixed reality to keep the player's real surroundings visible while placing the exercise interface and interactive elements in world space. This provides an alternative to performing exercises inside a completely virtual environment for folk who would otherwise get motion sick.

Designing VR Physiotherapy Minigames

I also explored how traditional game mechanics could be adapted into exercises while still encouraging the intended physical movements.


Archery Minigame

I designed an archery minigame where the player's left controller represents a bow whose string can be pulled by grabbing it with the right controller.

Pulling the string farther increases the shot's strength, encouraging the player to maintain a straight posture while performing the movement.

Looking back, I would add a left-handed setting so players could swap the bow and string-hand controls.


Sword Minigame

I designed a sword minigame where the player ought to stay near the center as much as possible while reaching for targets which fall to the ground.


Gamification

Aside from colours, VFX, SFX, maps and minigames, I also focused on focused on a streak system with the highest streaks available on the leaderboard.

Implementing a leaderboard for exercise scores would become competitive in an unhealthy way we felt.

Looking back, the UI would look more logical if the streak and leaderboard were put together considering their logic is connected. Also, your streak should probably start at "1" instead of "0" to give a fake feeling of jumpstarting your progression.

League of Legends Rune Concept: "Pick Your Poison"

I designed a rework for Time Warp Tonic because its value is both difficult to understand and difficult to defend against the other choices in the same row. Its 40% immediate healing can be valuable during the very first stage of laning, but after the first recall, Biscuit Delivery can provide comparable sustain while also granting up to 90 permanent maximum health.


My design goals & constraints:

  • I want this rune to remain a potion-based rune, as it would fit naturally between the other consumable runes: Biscuit Delivery and Triple Tonic.
  • I want to make this rune entirely early game focused. Not only because Time Warp Tonic is already established as an early game exclusive rune, but also because designing consumables for the mid-late game is pretty difficult as inventory space is limited.
  • I want this rune to have a high skill ceiling, as high skill ceiling runes are often the most fun runes to use, and also result in very interesting gameplay.
  • This rune exists in the "Inspiration" tree, which is mostly defined by creativity and unique tools. I want to make a rune that fits this theme by warping an already established system, similar to other runes in this tree like Hextech Flashtraption or Unsealed Spellbook.
  • I want the player choice of this rune to come in the form of a benefit/drawback system. I find such systems underrepresented in League, but I know that they can work, as Magical Footwear is a fairly respected rune in the same tree which denies players from buying their boots early.

Using these goals & constraints, I designed:


Pick Your Poison

Rune short description (hover):

"Choose between two potions at 3 min, then again 2 min later, and once more 2 min after that. Each potion offers a benefit at the cost of a drawback."

Rune long description (selected):

"Choose between Apple Juice and Orange Juice at 3 minutes. After drinking one, choose between Mango Juice and Pomegranate Juice 2 minutes later. After drinking one of those, choose one final time between Watermelon Juice and Ananas Juice 2 minutes later.

Each potion offers a benefit at the cost of a drawback for 30 seconds."

Functionality:

> When picking this rune, the player would receive a choice between two potions at minute 3. The two available potions temproarily occupy alternating inventory slots, making them the only usable item actives until the player drinks or sells one. The player's existing items and stats remain unchanged.

> The player then chooses whether they want to drink potion 1, drink potion 2, or sell the choice for 5g. Doing any of these actions immediately return the player's access to their inventory with appropriate SFX and/or small VFX feedback.

> Each potion provides a combat benefit and a combat drawback, meaning the player has to adapt their choice based on their current situation to get the most value out of this rune.

> After picking a potion, the player receives another pair of potions two minutes later. And after choosing from that pair, they receive one final pair two minutes after that.

> To prevent players from saving their potions too conservatively, the player has 30 seconds to drink a potion, or the game will decide for them (either randomly, or always the first in slot).


Potion concepts:

  • First potion choice (levels 3-4, min 3:00-3:30)
    • Apple Juice: "Increases damage by 10% but reduces healing received by 40% for 30s."
    • Orange Juice: "Increases healing received by 60% but reduces your damage by 5% for 30s."
  • Second potion choice (levels 4-6, min 5:00-6:00)
    • Mango Juice: "Increases ability haste by 30 but decreases attack speed by 15% for 30s."
    • Pomegranate Juice: "Increases attack speed by 30%, but decreases ability haste by 15 for 30s."
  • Third potion choice (levels 5-7, min 7:00-8:30)
    • Watermelon Juice: "Increases your movement speed by 40 but reduces your armor & magic resist by 10 for 30s."
    • Ananas Juice: "Increases all gold gained by 20% (max gain 300g) but reduces your movement speed by 10 for 30s."

Design decisions & thoughts

  • I made every juice represented by either red/pinkish fruit, or orange/yellow fruit. This way, they would be slightly easier to differentiate in the player's inventory during fights.
  • I made the decision for potions to fully occupy the player's inventory to force the player to drink the "poison".
    • This also solved the gameplay problem of a player having occupied 5-6 inventory slots, meaning there wouldn't be enough space to place the potions.
    • I still ended up allowing for the choices to be sold in base. That way, if a player wants to buy something and has absolutely no usecase for popping either potion; They can sell it to regain access to their inventory. This works similar to biscuits.
    • I was thinking of a high level play where players could purposefully hide their inventories from observant opponents, but that might be frustrating to play against at higher ranks, so it might be best if the potion choice is only manifested in your hotbar's inventory.
    • I don't think this inventory occupying system could work in a lategame potion system, as players will have very important item actives by then which should be immediately clickable at all times.
  • I don't think this inventory occupying system could work in the lategame, as players will have very important item actives by then which should be immediately clickable.
  • I was brainstorming the potential for toplaners to create counterplay for themselves in difficult or even impossible matchups. I also wanted something to be played around, no matter if it's you or the enemy that's using it. If your enemy has Pick Your Poison, each 30s timer would be a window of opportunity to abuse the potion's drawback or respect the potion's benefit. But the player drinking the potions will of course always be able to drink it more favourably.
  • I decided on three sets of potions. Not only because receiving more choices can make something more fun and engaging, but also because it would match the other runes in the same row, which both provide three consumables.
  • I gave the player 30s to make each decision, before the game decides for them. Had it been, lets say, 5 seconds; There would've been problems and restrict the potential skill ceiling of this rune:
    • Players who receive a potion choice while in base, wouldn't have a respectable window of opportunity to make use of the selected potion.
    • Players who experience occasional lagg spikes could miss the timing of popping the potion.
    • Players could purposefully pick a potion as early or as late as possible for any number of reasons, such as making use of the Ananas juice's gold amplifier more efficiently.
  • The statistics of this rune could be really fun to read and understand, as there might be champion mains who unexpectedly drink potion X over potion Y. Overall, if there's a potion that's heavily preferred over the other, it could be balanced appropiately by using the win/pickrate of that particular potion (as opposed to only looking at the entire rune's winrate).
  • I tried designing potions that didn't overlap too much with Biscuits or Tonics, such as a lumpsum gold bonus, adaptive force or max health driven potion. I also decided not to do anything with mana (or energy), as I personally believe rune availability should be spread as wide when possible across the champion roster to encourage creative player choices.
  • I'm aware that the structure of this rune can be considered complex for a medium-sized rune. I tried leaning towards the maximum allowed complexity budget that I would personally consider allowed. To compensate, I kept the individual potion effects simple and focused on (mostly) straightforward stat changes, allowing the complexity to come from the player's gameplay choices rather than from understanding each potion.
  • It's very much possible that this rune is useless in some games. I designed it for scenarios where the player expects combat heavy fights in the early game (which isn't something that happens in every game), no matter if the player's fighting chance is higher or lower compared to the enemy.

Potions reasoning

  • Apple Juice thought process:
    • This potion would be an option for players that need some extra damage in an early fight, and are willing to sacrifice their own early game sustainability to get it.
    • I estimated the average base health regen of all champions to be ~6.8 at level 3, which would regenerate 40 health over the span of 30 seconds.
    • Considering you receive the first potion pair at minute 3, it'd mean that the reduced healing should be balanced around health regen numbers and potions, as that is the primary source of healing for most champions in the early game. However, I don't consider making the antihealing specific for health regen as that would cause problems; A lot of champions' early game sustain rely mostly on lifesteal starter items, and Briar wouldn't receive any drawbacks considering she already has no base health regen in her kit.
    • Another edge case could be fountain healing. If you were to recall while under this potion's effect, you might have a hard time regenerating health before you could walk back to base. That's why I would also suggest giving the fountain "pure healing", similar to it's already "pure damage" mechanic. "Pure healing" would ignore grievous wounds effects, healing the player like normal. However, I am aware that giving the fountain "pure healing" could influence champion balancing with global ults that have bought grievous wounds (i.e. Karthus). So if giving the fountain pure healing isn't an option, I would consider changing the antiheal effect to be specific to selfhealing.
    • It's also worth noting that junglers could use this potion to increase their clear speed by a couple extra seconds without any drawback (as junglers often care less about staying healthy during their clears), but most junglers would then have to give up either Cosmic Insight (-13.73s on smite), or cash back (200g–1000g). Assuming the trend persists of the Inspiration tree being primarily used as a secondary tree for junglers.
    • To discourage players from popping a potion while under this effect, I had the idea to add a barf SFX, but if players would understand why their character barfed remains to be tested.
    • ADCs should avoid this potion when they have a healing support or if either the ADC or Support has the Heal summoner spell available. This also makes me consider adding The greavous wounds symbol while under the effect to communicate to the support that their healing abilities will be weaker.
  • Orange Juice thought process:
    • This potion would be an option for players that are looking for some sustain in a difficult matchup, and are willing to sacrifice their own damage output to get it.
    • In a way, this potion works similar to Time Warp Tonic, as it amplifies the healing from drinking potions.
    • Using the same numbers from earlier, this potion would heal ~30 extra health for base health/regen, and heal an extra 72 when using a potion while under it's effect. Not counting other forms of sustain such as lifesteal, healing runes, heal summoner spell, or healing abilities.
    • Orange juice could create some high-level strategic decisions when using your other forms of sustain. You might be in need of a potion at 2:45, but you could consider waiting until 3:00 before popping the potion to gain that extra healing. You could imagine similar situations for using your healing abilities (like vladimir Q) or runes (Fleet footwork) effectively.
    • It's important to take in mind that the damage debuff would reduce lifesteal and omnivamp when balancing the numbers of this potion.
  • Mango & Pomegranate Juices thought process:
    • The Mango Juice would be an option for players that want some extra abilities in an extended fight, and are willing to sacrifice their attack speed to cast them.
    • The Pomegranate Juice would be an option for players that need an extra few auto attacks in a fight, and are willing to sacrifice their abilities to do so.
    • According to the League wiki, 2 ability haste == 1 attack speed in gold value. However, this is much different in the earlygame, where auto attacks are a bigger chunk of any champion's damage dealt. The early game is also a time where abilities are weaker and cost a bigger budget of your mana, making the haste worth less and the attack speed worth more.
    • I wanted both of these potions to be slightly safer choices compared to the other sets of choices, simply as an intermission between the two more lane defining options.
    • There are champions that heavily value one of these stats over the other, such as mages picking mango, and attack-heavy ADCs picking Pomegranate. Despite that, there are quite a few champions that might contemplate which one they'd want for the current situation, such as Jax, Diana, Katarina, Ezreal, Lucian, and more.
  • Watermelon Juice thought process:
    • This potion would be an option for players that either need to get to lane/objective/camp/gank quicker, or want to have more success dodging or running away from someone, and are willing to sacrifice their durability to do so.
    • This potion is pretty flexible, because movement speed is simply useful for many different situations.
    • Reducing your resistances at this stage of the game can be pretty detrimental, as assasins often have some lethality at this stage of the game.
  • Ananas Juice thought process:
    • This potion would be an option for players who know that they will generate a bunch of gold soon, and are willing to sacrifice their movement to be able to generate it.
    • I wanted this last set of potions to be very difficult to pick between.
    • Picking Ananas might seem the overall more consistent option, but the player has to consider if the movement speed loss could potentially reduce the gold they'd gain, in a similar way when people decide to pick/not pick Magical Footwear.
    • I consider this potion especially fun, because it's a fun challenge to maximize your gold gained during the next 30 seconds.
    • Every non-support player gains 61.2 gold passively over 30s. To reach the maximum of 300g, you would have to gain an extra (1500g - 61.2g) during the next 30s.
    • This potion might not be picked as often on supports, as their gold generated is lower on average.
    • This potion could be balanced around making it more consistent, so increasing the gold percentage but decreasing the gold cap.
    • Selling items obviously shouldn't count towards gold gained, that would otherwise be a disaster when undoing bought items.

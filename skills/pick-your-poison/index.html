League of Legends Rune Concept

I designed a rework for Time Warp Tonic, as I believe its value is too niche and difficult for players to understand, limiting the Inspiration tree's potential from offering more interesting strategic choices.


My design goals & constraints:

  • I want this rune to remain a potion-based rune, as it would fit naturally between the other consumable runes: Biscuit Delivery and Triple Tonic.
  • I want to make this rune entirely early game focused. Not only because the current Time Warp Tonic is already established as a near-early game exclusive rune, but also because the Triple Tonic and Biscuit Delivery runes are already covering the early-mid game transition. And designing consumables for the mid-late game is pretty difficult as inventory space is limited.
  • Rather than an advantage that's difficult to perceive, I want to create an advantage that's easier to gauge with clear player choices & strategic depth. I want to achieve this by warping an already established system, similar to other runes in the Inspiration tree like Hextech Flashtraption.
  • I want the player choice of this rune to come in the form of a benefit/drawback system. I find such systems underrepresented in League, but I know that they can work, as Magical Footwear is a fairly respected rune in the same tree which denies players from buying their boots early.

Using these goals & constraints, I designed:


"Pick Your Poison"

Rune description:

"Choose between two potions at 3 min, then again 2 min later, and once more 2 min after that. Each potion offers a combat benefit at the cost of a drawback."

Functionality:

> When picking this rune, the player would receive a choice between two potions at minute 3. The two available potions temproarily occupy alternating inventory slots, making them the only usable item actives until the player drinks or sells one. The player's existing items and stats remain unchanged.

> The player then chooses whether they want to drink potion 1, drink potion 2, or sell it for 10g. Doing any of these actions immediately return the player's access to their inventory with appropriate SFX and/or VFX feedback.

> Each potion provides a combat benefit and a combat drawback, meaning the player has to adapt their choice based on their current situation to get the most value out of this rune.

> After picking a potion, the player receives another pair of potions two minutes later. And after choosing from that pair, they receive one final pair two minutes after that.

> To prevent players from saving their potions too conservatively, the player has 30 seconds to drink a potion, or the game will decide for them (either randomly, or always the first in slot).


Potion concepts:

  • First potion choice (levels 3-4, min ~3:05)
    • Apple Juice: "Increases damage by 10% but reduces healing received by 80% for 30s."
    • Orange Juice: "Increases healing by 50% but reduces your damage by 10% for 30s."
  • Second potion choice (levels 4-6, min ~5:10)
    • Mango Juice: "Increases ability haste by 15 but decreases attack speed by 30% for 30s."
    • Pomegranate Juice: "Increases attack speed by 30%, but decreases ability haste by 15 for 30s."
  • Third potion choice (levels 5-7, min ~7:15)
    • Watermelon Juice: "Increases your movement speed by 30 but reduces your armor & magic resist by 15 for 30s."
    • Ananas Juice: "Increases all gold gained to 120% (up to 300g) but reduces your movement speed by 15 for 30s."

Other design decisions

  • I made every choice represented by either red/pinkish fruit, or orange/yellow fruit. This way, they would be slightly easier to differentiate in the player's inventory during fights.
  • I was brainstorming the potential for toplaners to create counterplay for themselves in difficult or even impossible matchups. I also wanted something to be played around, no matter if it's you or the enemy that's using it. If your enemy has Pick Your Poison, each 30s timer would be a window of opportunity to abuse the potion's drawback or respect the potion's benefit. But the player drinking the potions will of course always be able to drink it more favourably.
  • I decided on three sets of potions. Not only because receiving more choices can make something more fun and engaging, but also because it would match the other runes in the same row, which both provide three consumables.
  • I allowed for the potions to be sold. That way, if a player wants to buy something in base and has quite literally no usecase for popping either potion; They can sell it to gain access to their inventory.
  • I'm aware that the structure of this rune might be too complex for a medium-sized rune. To compensate, I kept the individual potion effects simple and focused on (mostly) straightforward stat changes, allowing the complexity to come from the player's choices rather than from understanding each potion (complexity budgeting).
  • I tried designing potions that didn't overlap too much with Biscuits or Tonics, such as a lumpsum gold bonus, adaptive force or max health driven potion. I also decided not to do anything with mana (or energy), as I personally believe rune availability should be spread as wide when possible across the champion roster to encourage creative player choices.
  • It's very much possible that this rune is useless in some games. I designed it for scenarios where the player expects combat heavy fights in the early game (which isn't something that happens in every game), no matter if the player's fighting chance is higher or lower compared to the enemy.

Potion reasoning

  • Apple Juice thought process:
    • This potion would be an option for players that need some extra damage in an early fight, and are willing to sacrifice their own early game sustainability to get it.
    • The average base health regen of all champions is 6.8 at level 3, which would regenerate 40 health over the span of 30 seconds. Not counting any lifesteal doran items, healing runes, or healing abilities, this potion will reduce the average champion's base healing for at least 30 health.
    • Considering you receive the first potion pair at minute 3, it'd mean that the reduced healing should be balanced around these health regen numbers, as that is the primary source of healing for all champions in the early game. However, I don't consider making the -80% antihealing specific for health regen as that would cause problems; A lot of champions' early games also rely on lifesteal starter items, and Briar wouldn't receive any drawbacks considering she already has no base health regen in her kit.
    • Another edge case could be fountain healing. If you were to recall while under this potion's effect, you might have a hard time regenerating health before you could walk back to base. That's why I would also suggest giving the fountain "pure healing", similar to it's already "pure damage" mechanic. "Pure healing" would ignore grievous wounds effects, healing the player like normal. However, I am aware that giving the fountain "pure healing" could influence champion balancing with global ults that have bought grievous wounds (i.e. Karthus). So if giving the fountain pure healing isn't an option, I would consider changing the -80% antiheal effect to be specific to selfhealing.
    • It's also worth noting that junglers could use this potion to increase their clear speed by potentially 2-4 seconds faster without any drawback, (as most of the time, they don't really care about the reduced healing), but most junglers would then have to give up either Cosmic Insight (-13.73s on smite), or cash back (200g–1000g). Assuming the trend persists of the Inspiration tree being primarily used as a secondary tree for junglers.
  • Orange Juice thought process:
    • This potion would be an option for players that are looking for some sustain in a difficult matchup, and are willing to sacrifice their own damage output to get it.
    • Using the same numbers from earlier, this potion would heal for at least 20 extra health on average, not counting other forms of sustain such as red/refillable potions, lifesteal, healing runes, or healing abilities.
    • Orange juice could create some high-level strategic decisions when using your other forms of sustain. You might be in need of a potion at 2:45, but you could consider waiting until 3:00 before popping the potion to gather an extra 50%. You could imagine similar situations for using your healing abilities (like vladimir Q) or runes (Fleet footwork) effectively.
    • It's important to take in mind that the -10% damage debuff would reduce lifesteal and omnivamp when balancing the numbers of this potion.
  • Mango & Pomegranate Juices thought process:
    • The Mango Juice would be an option for players that want some extra abilities in an extended fight, and are willing to sacrifice their attack speed to cast them.
    • The Pomegranate Juice would be an option for players that need an extra few auto attacks in a fight, and are willing to sacrifice their abilities to do so.
    • I wanted both of these potions to be slightly safer choices compared to the other sets of choices, simply as an intermission between the two more lane defining options.
    • There are champions that heavily value one of these stats over the other, such as mages picking mango, and attack-heavy ADCs picking Pomegranate. Despite that, there are quite a few champions that might contemplate which one they'd want for the current situation, such as Jax, Diana, Katarina, Ezreal, Lucian, and more.
  • Watermelon Juice thought process:
    • This potion would be an option for players that either need to get to lane/objective/camp/gank quicker, or want to have more success dodging or running away from someone, and are willing to sacrifice their durability to do so.
    • This potion is pretty flexible, because movement speed is simply useful for many different situations.
  • Ananas Juice thought process:
    • This potion would be an option for players who know that they will generate a bunch of gold soon, and are willing to sacrifice their movement to be able to generate it.
    • I wanted this last set of potions to be very difficult to pick between.
    • Picking Ananas might seem the overall more consistent option, but the player has to consider if the movement speed loss would even reduce the gold they'd gain, in a similar way when people decide to pick Magical Footwear.
    • I consider this potion especially fun, because it's a fun challenge to maximize your gold gained during the next 30 seconds.
    • Every player gains 61.2 gold passively over 30s. To reach the maximum of 300g, you would have to gain a total of 1500g during the next 30s.
    • This potion might not be picked as often on supports, as their gold generated is lower on average.
    • This potion could be balanced around making it more consistent, so increasing the gold percentage but decreasing the gold cap.
    • Selling items obviously shouldn't count towards gold gained, that would otherwise be a disaster when undoing.

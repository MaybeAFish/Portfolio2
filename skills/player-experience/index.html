Player Experience

In the context of games, the player experience (PE) is how the player feels, thinks or reacts while playing the game.

Analyzing this experience properly is a very time-consuming task, as it usually takes many iterations and playtests to understand if and why a game system sucks. It's also very difficult because evaluating the player experience requires the viewpoint of players, but players aren't game designers. They could be conflating one system's problems for the one you're testing, or they could be explaining the wrong reasons why something is fun or frustrating.

But since it takes a lot of resources to explore the player experience, it means that Game designers must be very selective on which systems they want to analyze in depth (depending on team size).

These are some of the player experience problems I personally chose to investigate and improve:


Doomed

Melee

Melee attacks initially lacked any weight and feedback nor was it visible in the HUD. Not a single player was using this mechanic. I improved the timing, animation, sound, damage system and enemy knockback so that hits felt more consistent and impactful.

Weapon Swapping

I added a slow motion effect for the weaponwheel. But during playtests, I noticed that experienced players would try using the number keys instead of the weapon wheel. I added 1-4 weapon switching because it better matched how players actually wanted to swap between their weapons.

Projectiles

Enemy projectiles could originally pass through walls and kill players without giving them a reasonable opportunity to understand wtf happened. I explored reflecting projectiles off the environment instead, which ended up being way more fun to play versus.

Reloading

Playtests revealed frustration when players unexpectedly had to manually reload before they could continue fighting. I changed the reload behaviour so weapons would automatically reload whenever it made sense.

Onboarding

I adjusted enemy placement, spacing and tutorial prompts so players learned movement and combat mechanics in a better more deliberate way.

Ability Readability

Players had no clue how the abilities worked. I designed and implemented ability learning, added VFX around the weapons and created HUD elements to make it more clear.


Tetris Airconsole

During interal playtests, players encountered confusing situations when someone disconnected, reconnected, or joined at an unexpected moment. These situations would interrupt the game or leave players unsure about what was happening.

I treated these as player experience problems rather than technical problems. I designed and proposed the desired behaviour to the Creative Director before we'd start discussing the details, including reconnect timers, pausing behaviour, multiple disconnected players and joining during different game states.


Karaoke Game

I designed a Play of the Game system for a karaoke game by performing the following steps:

  1. Identifying the different motivations people might enjoy while playing karaoke.
  2. Determining which in-game recorded events could realistically represent those motivations.
  3. Determining the weights of those events.
  4. Determining how the weights should be used when calculating what the moment should be.
  5. Implementing it, and then tons of playtesting to make some number adjustments.

Research is OP

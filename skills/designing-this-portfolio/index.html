My Design Reasoning Behind this Portfolio

The main idea behind my portfolio

I wanted this portfolio to not waste any time.

  • I made my portfolio's homepage the projects page.
  • This page has a small banner with only the most necessary information like my title & socials, with some more information available on hover.
  • At it's highest level, I explain my projects using media carousels with not a lot of text.
  • Where there was text, I tried writing as little generic portfolio language when possible.

However, this "show, don't tell" approach isn't a rule you can forever rely on when selling your game design skills, which is something that needs to be well explained.

I tried saving all of my design thinking & explaining exactly 2 clicks deep, while any generic info remaining 1 click deep.

Projects

I wanted my projects page to communicate that my game design and game development skills are connected and influence each other, rather than separating them completely.

Doomed

I tried achieving this by starting off with a project that strongly showcases all of my skills. Design thinking, player experience, game systems and more.

Tetris Airconsole

The tetris game i worked on is a shipped project, where I had done some nice polishing work on, which is why i put that second. I had designed multiplayer stability features and implemented various UI, UX, Interaction and animations.

Vulkan Game Engine

I put the game engine last, which is where I plan on putting all of my coding skills. I believe that someone's programming skills can be mostly shown with one strong project, while with game design skills it needs to be proven that you're flexible which is why almost all of my portfolio, including the skills page, is mostly game design work. game design skills neeed to be able to applyable to different things.

Project Gallary

I have made a ton of other projects which I'm also proud of, but aren't part of my top 3. I was also contemplating on puting a game in my portfolio, similar to other famous game dev portfolio's like xyz with a car game. I had the idea to showcase my design skills through this interactive showcase, where you can explore and find my other projects. I had initially called this "The Underground" as a game name, but I noticed people getting confused when entering it, wondering what it's for. "Project Gallary" ended up being understood clearer.

Articles titles

My article titles used to be very boring. This was before I heard people mention to sell your "impact" on your portfolio. I tried achieving this by creating more interesting title articles by thinking what I actually achieved with my work.

  • From "Creating Multiplayer Stability in a Tetris Game" to "Reducing Player Frustration in Tetris Airconsole"
  • From "Designing Gamified Physiotherapy Exercises in VR" to "Making Physiotherapy Exercises Fun"
  • From "Iterating on Doomed's HUD" to "Making Doomed's Visuals Work Together"
  • From "Improving Doomed's Onboarding" to "Making Doomed Easier to Learn"
  • From "Polishing Doomed's Combat with Playtesting" to "Making Doomed's Combat Feel Better"
  • From "Designing a Play of the Game for a Karaoke Game" to "Finding the Most Fun 15 Seconds of a Karaoke Performance"
  • From "Environmental Hazards" to "Forcing the Player to Reposition in Interesting Ways"

I realized that I could shape the entire thought process people have when reading my articles, by simply changing the title even though the content remaining the same.

My Design Reasoning Behind this Portfolio

The Main Idea

I wanted this portfolio to not waste any time.

  • I made my portfolio's homepage the projects page.
  • This page has a small banner with only the most necessary information like my title & socials, with some more information available on hover.
  • At its highest level, I explain my projects using media carousels with not a lot of text.
  • Where text was necessary, I tried to make it specific to my work rather than filling it with generic portfolio language.
  • I kept the background visually repetitive so it wouldn't compete for attention with the main work.

However, this "show, don't tell" approach isn't a rule you can forever rely on when selling your game design skills, as that is something which needs to be explained well.

I kept most of my detailed design thinking exactly 2 clicks deep, while keeping generic information 1 click deep.


Projects Page

I wanted my projects page to communicate that my game design and game development skills are connected and influence each other, rather than separating them completely.

  • Doomed

    I started with a project that showcases my best skills simultaneously: design thinking, player experience, game systems and implementation.

  • Tetris Airconsole

    I placed Tetris second because it is a shipped project and shows both player experience work, and implementation work across UX, feedback and animations.

  • Vulkan Game Engine

    I placed the engine last because I wanted my technical work to be visible without letting it dominate the portfolio. One strong technical project gives me a place to demonstrate lower-level programming and system architecture, while the rest of the portfolio can show how I apply design thinking across very different projects.

  • Project Gallary

    I also wanted a way to include my other projects without giving them the same importance as my three main projects. This made me create an interactive "Project Gallery", where visitors can explore additional work themselves in a fun way.

    I originally called it "The Underground" because I treated it like a small game. In practice, people entering the gallery often did not understand what they were looking at or why they were there. I changed the name to "Project Gallery" as communicating the function of the experience is more important than a cool name.

Skills Page

I faced a lot of problems when designing the skills page's layout.

My first iteration included a couple blocks in a grid, where each block represent one specific skill.

However, I realized that this isn't a great way of showing one's skills, because skills influence each other. A game mechanic might exist to reach a player experience goal, or a change to the pacing might be necessary to make that mechanic understandable.

This is why I ended up with a "Skills Evidence Library": a collection of articles where each article is linked to one or more filterable skills.

My article titles used to be very boring, descriptive titles. This was before I realized that readers wanted to read about my "impact" instead. I tried achieving this by writing more interesting titles by thinking what I actually achieved with my work:

  • From "Designing Gamified Physiotherapy Exercises in VR" to "Making Physiotherapy Exercises Fun"
  • From "Creating Multiplayer Stability in a Tetris Game" to "Reducing Player Frustration in Tetris Airconsole"
  • From "Environmental Hazards" to "Forcing the Player to Reposition in Interesting Ways"
  • From "Iterating on Doomed's HUD" to "Making Doomed's Visuals Work Together"
  • From "Improving Doomed's Onboarding" to "Making Doomed Easier to Learn"
  • From "Polishing Doomed's Combat with Playtesting" to "Making Doomed's Combat Feel Better"
  • From "Designing a Play of the Game for a Karaoke Game" to "Finding the Most Fun 15 Seconds of a Karaoke Performance"

I realized that I could shape the entire thought process people have when reading my articles by simply changing the title, despite the content remaining the same.

Why I Made This Article

I consider this portfolio another showcase of my design skills, as it answers and solves some design problems:

  • What should the user see first?
  • What information is actually necessary?
  • How do I guide attention to this information?
  • How do I show my impact when the viewer only has a few seconds?

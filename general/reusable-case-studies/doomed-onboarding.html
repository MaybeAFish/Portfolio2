Making Doomed Easier to Learn

Why was Doomed hard to learn at first?

I identified several problems with Doomed's initial combat system. It's biggest issue was the lack of gradual learning:

You spawned in the game with 4 weapons and 4 abilities. Deciding on the weapons was a quick decision, we made the player spawn with only the pistol, with the other weapons scattered across the map.

The abilities however, required a bit more design thinking.


Reworking Abilities

One of my design arguments was to remove the mana system and make abilities consume ammo instead.

Mana was an additional resource that wasn't really contributing anything meaningful to the experience. It felt overcomplicated nor did it feel very fun to manage.

Another problem was the amount of inputs the game had. Every single ability had it's own key.

So I decided to couple the abilities and weapons together. Each weapon would have its own right-click ability, which the player could unlock by collecting a corresponding "rune". This way, not only would 4 inputs be reduced to 1, it would also allow for abilities to cost ammo instead of mana which can lead to much more interesting gameplay decisions.

I implemented this ability learning system and also suggested giving each weapon a VFX aura. My idea was to make it immediately understandable which weapons already had an active ability associated with them.

VFXs not made by me, only positioned them.


Matching weapons with abilities

I also decided on which ability should belong to which weapon. I wanted each combination to give the weapon a reason to exist within the overall combat loop.

  • Pistol + Frost

    Frost was intended to be the strongest crowd-control ability. The pistol is also the weapon players have the least reason to use once they have access to the other, stronger and more fun weapons. Giving it the strongest ability creates an incentive to occasionally switch back to it.

  • Shotgun + Void

    Void was designed as a powerful one-time burst with a long cooldown. The shotgun already had limited ammo and was more situational (close range), making it a great match for an ability that the player saves for an important moment.

  • Minigun + Flames

    Flames was the only ability without a cooldown. Instead, it consumed ammo the fastest because each flame projectile costs an ammo. Since the minigun has 100 ammo, the combination already naturally supports the fantasy of firing a minigun into a big horde of enemies.

  • Rifle + Shock

    The rifle is the "bread-and-butter" weapon and is likely to be used the most during combat. Shock was designed around a relatively low cooldown and low cost, which encourages frequent use as it's very cost-efficient. It felt right putting the most frequently used ability on the most frequently used weapon. Especially considering it was the only combination left..

The ability system could still use more polish. Specifically, the ability projectiles should visibly originate from the weapon barrel, and it could also make good use of a short casting animation so it feels more connected to the held weapons.


Onboarding

I decided on various enemy placements to simplify the learning curve. The game immediately started off with 3 artillery enemies, which are a significant level complexer compared to the skeleton enemies. Also, the current starter island didn't provide a strong enough sense of direction to the player.

The loadingscreen already had a cool looking bridge, so I decided to implement that same bridge as the starter bridge of the game.

  • I put a single skeleton near the end of this bridge
  • I put 2 skeletons and 1 artillery on the first island
  • and I put 3 skeletons and 2 artilleries on the second island

This greatly improved the difficulty curve of the game.

I also proposed the idea to enlarge the distance between islands to force the player to learn the double jump and dash mechanics. Using the bridge skeleton, I made sure the player had to learn basic combat mechanics before they had to learn advanced movement.

The game already had UI info popups to explain mechanics like these, but I improved on them by placing their hitboxes more deliberate in locations where it was actually necessary to learn them. I made the popups last longer and I reduced the text inside them to be more concise.

And finally, I strategically placed some map elements to nudge the player in the right direction (breadcrumbing) using already available assets like health pots and statues.
